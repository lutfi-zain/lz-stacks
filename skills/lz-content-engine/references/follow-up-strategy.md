# Follow-Up Strategy

A framework to capitalize on content wins. When a post outperforms, the audience has validated the topic. This strategy details how to turn a single hit into a sequence of compounding posts instead of starting from zero.

## The Principle of Audience Signals
High performance (shares, saves, high comment volume) is a direct instruction from your audience. Instead of jumping to a new topic, extract the core signal and build follow-ups.

---

## The 5 Follow-Up Angles
From any winning post, generate content across these five distinct angles:

### 1. The Deeper Cut
Go one level deeper on the exact mechanism or sub-topic that drove the win.
- *Example:* If a post on "3 visual rules for carousels" wins, the deeper cut is: "How I pick the exact display font for my headlines."

### 2. The Next Question
Identify the obvious "but what about..." question the audience raised in the comments.
- *Example:* "But how do you handle background images without breaking the 3-color rule?"

### 3. The Other Side
Provide a contrarian or complementary view.
- *Example:* "When the 3-color rule fails: 3 artistic exceptions to the rule."

### 4. The How-To / What-Is (The Inverse)
If the winning post was high-level theory ("what"), create a tactical walkthrough ("how"). If it was tactical, create the high-level philosophy.
- *Example:* "A step-by-step video of me applying the visual system in Figma in 5 minutes."

### 5. The Proof (Case Study)
Share real-world evidence, results, or a client case study that validates the claim made in the original post.
- *Example:* "How my client used this visual system to grow from 1.2k to 10k followers in 30 days."

---

## Execution Workflow
1. **Analyze comments:** Copy and paste the comment section from the winning post.
2. **Identify the primary signal:** What specific sub-topic or line are people replying to most?
3. **Map the 5 angles:** Draft titles, formats, and climax-led hooks.
4. **Select the immediate next post:** Pick the highest-leverage angle (usually the Deeper Cut or Next Question) and write it immediately while the original is still active.
