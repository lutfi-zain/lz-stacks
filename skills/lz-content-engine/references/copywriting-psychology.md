# Copywriting Psychology

A research-backed reference on the psychological principles and copywriting frameworks that drive attention, retention, and action on social media.

## 1. Foundational Frameworks

### AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)
- **Attention:** Hook the reader immediately (pattern interrupt or bold claim).
- **Interest:** Explain the context or problem. Make it personal to the target persona.
- **Desire:** Show the solution and the ideal future state (the "after").
- **Action:** Provide a single, friction-free next step.

### PAS (Problem, Agitation, Solution)
- **Problem:** State a frustrating reality the reader faces.
- **Agitation:** Make them feel the consequences of ignoring the problem.
- **Solution:** Introduce your method or tool as the escape path.

### BAB (Before, After, Bridge)
- **Before:** Describe their current frustrating state.
- **After:** Describe the dream result where the problem is solved.
- **Bridge:** Reveal how to get from Before to After (your system or product).

## 2. Hook Frameworks
The five most effective patterns to open a post:
1. **The Curiosity Gap:** State a result but withhold the mechanism.
   - *Example:* "I built an API that makes $200/day. The code is only 40 lines."
2. **The Pattern Interrupt:** Challenge a universally accepted industry standard.
   - *Example:* "Stop writing READMEs. Write AGENTS.md instead."
3. **The Bold Claim:** A shocking or dramatic statistic/assertion.
   - *Example:* "90% of developers fail their system design interviews because of one diagram."
4. **The Story Open:** Start right in the middle of a high-stakes moment.
   - *Example:* "I sat in the meeting room as the database crashed. 14,000 users were online."
5. **The Diagnostic Question:** Target a specific pain point directly.
   - *Example:* "Does your codebase take more than 10 minutes to spin up locally?"

## 3. Cognitive Biases in Content
- **Loss Aversion:** People are more motivated to avoid losing $100 than they are to gain $100. Highlight mistakes, traps, and wasted time.
- **Social Proof:** Show case studies, user numbers, and screenshots of real engagement.
- **Authority Bias:** Reference your personal experience, years in the industry, or client metrics to establish trust early.

---

## 4. CTA Psychology
- **The Single CTA Principle:** Giving the user multiple options (e.g., "Follow me, retweet, and check out my website") results in choice paralysis. They will do none. Always pick ONE primary action.
- **Friction Reduction:** Make the action simple. E.g., *"Comment 'FLOW' to get the PDF"* is lower friction than *"Go to my bio, click the link, input your email, and check your spam folder."*
- **The Value-Led Close:** Don't sell. Offer the next logical step that continues the value of the post.
